Computation limits

Start with the defaults. If a mutation hits a limit, its label tells you which one. You can raise that limit and compute again; larger limits may need more time or memory.

Max module dimension limits the sum of the three numbers in a module’s dimension vector. For example, (2, 3, 1) has total dimension 6.

Stored matrix entries limits the size of the linear-algebra calculations. Raising it allows larger calculations, but uses more memory.

Extension rounds limits how many times a single mutation can enlarge a module while searching for its result. Some mutations need several rounds; others may never finish.

If one mutation hits a size or round limit, the app skips that direction and tries the others. The time limit and Stop computation end the whole search and keep the results found so far.

Resource limit means the app could not finish that calculation. It does not mean the mutation is mathematically impossible.

Additional safeguards

The app also limits each search to 1,200 chambers and checks for very large exact coefficients during elimination (over 2,500 digits in a pivot row). For sparse matrices, the storage limit counts nonzero entries; for dense matrices, it counts all entries. Entries created during calculation count too.

Chamber inequalities & rays
Reading the views & conventions

Chambers and mutations. Each graph node is a chamber. An edge joins two chambers related by one mutation. The fan shows the corresponding regions in stability space. Click a chamber in either view to see its labels.

Distance. This is the number of mutation steps from your chosen base region, counting steps in either direction. For example, distance 2 asks for chambers reachable in at most two steps. If some calculations are unfinished, a shorter route to a displayed chamber may still be missing.

Brick labels. Each chamber has three labels, one for each mutation direction. The tuple (d₁, d₂, d₃) gives the brick’s vector-space dimension at each quiver vertex. Different bricks can have the same tuple: choose Inspect to see the arrow matrices that distinguish them.

Upwards and downwards. These describe the mutation direction, not the label’s position on screen. Graph arrows point in the upwards direction. The base selector starts you with all three labels upwards or all three downwards.

Reading the fan. The sphere shows where each chamber meets a sphere centered at the origin. Rotate it to see the other side. The slice view cuts the chambers with a plane; it is available when all displayed chamber rays meet that plane in their positive direction.

Mathematical notation and scope

Write a two-term simple-minded collection as D ∪ U[1], where D contains the downwards labels and U contains the upwards labels before shifting. Form C with the dimension vectors of D as positive columns and those of U as negative columns.

A stability parameter θ lies in the displayed cone when Cᵀθ ≥ 0. The columns of C⁻ᵀ are its ray directions. These inequalities and rays appear above under Chamber inequalities & rays.

The computation starts from the three vertex simple modules and explores their finite extensions. These are nilpotent representations: all sufficiently long paths act as zero. The algebra itself may be infinite-dimensional; the app does not impose a fixed maximum path length or require admissible relations.
